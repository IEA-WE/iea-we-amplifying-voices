International Economic Association · Women in Economics

Strengthening the presence and influence of women in economics.

IEA-WE is a global initiative strengthening the presence and influence of women in the economics profession — through research, collaboration, advocacy, and amplifying women's voices in global economic discourse.

Why this matters

Economics shapes the world.

Economics shapes how we understand governments, markets, and public policy — yet women remain underrepresented, particularly in leadership roles. These disparities deepen across race, ethnicity, and geography, with women from developing countries often facing a double disadvantage.

IEA-WE takes an intersectional approach, examining how gender intersects with race, caste, and region to identify patterns of multiple disadvantage.

Diversity strengthens the discipline. Greater inclusion isn't only a matter of fairness — diverse perspectives lead to richer analysis, more innovative solutions, and better-functioning societies.

A global community of women economists. Across Africa, Asia and Latin America, IEA-WE connects researchers, policymakers, and institutions working to strengthen women's participation and influence in economics.

01 — RESEARCH & INTERVENTIONS

Research & Interventions

Six evidence studies — across Argentina, Colombia, Ghana, India, Mexico and South Africa — have been completed and published, building a comprehensive foundation for understanding the structural barriers women economists face. Alongside this, eleven intervention projects are underway testing practical strategies to close the gender gap and grow the number of women who choose economics as a field of study and career. This is complemented by over 220 in-depth interviews and 100+ focus group discussions led by Monica Biradavolu of QualAnalytics.

About IEA-WE

Advancing equity and excellence in economics.

The International Economic Association launched IEA-WE as a multi-year global initiative to strengthen the presence and influence of women in the economics profession.

Through research, collaboration and advocacy, the initiative works to expand the pipeline of women economists, remove structural barriers to their advancement, and amplify their voices in shaping global economic discourse — with particular attention to the intersecting disadvantages of geography, race and ethnicity.

Led by economists Ashwini Deshpande, María Inés Berniell, Raquel Fernández, Dani Rodrik and Fiona Tregenna, IEA-WE operates across Asia, Africa, and Latin America and the Caribbean.

We gratefully acknowledge the support of the Co-Impact Foundation and the Open Society Foundation, whose funding has made this initiative possible.
