Executive Summary

An editorial bridge between research and the real world.

Launched in 2023, Amplifying Voices set out to broaden who gets to shape global policy conversations. It's grown from a scrappy pilot into IEA-WE's primary mechanism for getting evidence in front of policymakers, journalists, and the public — one carefully edited, carefully placed article at a time.

Nearly every piece published features an author based in Africa, Asia, or Latin America and the Caribbean — regions where women economists remain structurally underrepresented in the outlets that shape policy debate. Run as a one-person editorial operation by Navika Mehta, the project pairs hands-on mentoring with strategic media partnerships and full-cycle publication support.

— why this work matters —

The Gap

The mic hasn't been shared equally.

Women — especially those based in low- and middle-income countries — still face outsized barriers to publishing, entering public debate, and influencing the policy conversations that affect them most. That means the evidence base policymakers actually see is missing perspectives it needs.

“

Public debate is stronger when it draws on a wide range of expertise and lived experience.

— Why This Work Matters

Amplifying Voices helps close that gap by translating rigorous research into accessible, policy-ready commentary and pairing it with the platforms that can carry it furthest. Contributors work across labour markets, gender equality, climate, trade, fiscal policy, education, and development — and many publish their first-ever policy commentary through the project before going on to write independently for leading outlets. It's a visibility project. It's also a pipeline.

How It Works

Four stages. One editorial relationship.

Sourcing, matching, mentoring, dissemination — the same four moves, run on repeat, for every single contributor.

01

Sourcing contributors

Institutional pages are outdated, inboxes go unanswered — finding women economists across Asia, Africa, and Latin America takes persistent, direct outreach. A second route runs through the journals themselves, though the discipline's own gender gap means it surfaces fewer names than the labour-intensive route does.

02

Matching research to platform

Each piece gets pitched to the right home — VoxDev, VoxEU, or Project Syndicate — each with its own bar for entry. VoxDev and VoxEU select from top journals, a built-in quality filter. Project Syndicate runs on scarcity and timing: outreach targets research that connects to a live, high-visibility debate, from AI-driven layoffs to gender gaps in male-dominated fields.

03

Editorial mentoring

One-to-one guidance, tailored to each author's voice and experience — turning rigorous research into clear commentary without flattening the analysis. Many return to write again, which is the point.

04

Dissemination

Publication isn't the finish line. Every piece gets pushed through the Substack newsletter and social channels, extending reach well past the partner platform's own audience.

Editorial Priority

Built for the Global South, from day one.

Nearly every article published features an author based in Africa, Asia, or Latin America and the Caribbean. That's not incidental — it's the project's founding premise, and it's required real work: in parts of Africa especially, low visibility and fragmented institutional data mean contributors are found through sustained, proactive outreach rather than open calls.

IEA-WE's own research shows persistent gender gaps in academic publishing — gaps that ripple into who clears the bar at outlets like VoxEU and VoxDev. So the project has built new pathways around that constraint, including a country-focused series with VoxDev that pairs the wider research literature with deep national context.

Peer Effects

Publication isn't the exit — it's the intro.

Almost every published economist comes back with two or three referrals — colleagues whose research they think deserves the same platform. That organic, word-of-mouth network has become one of the project's strongest sources of new contributors, and the foundation for the community IEA-WE is now building on purpose.

The network also includes North–South collaborations, with some articles co-authored alongside economists based in Europe and North America — connecting research across regions rather than treating it as a one-way pipeline.

— what we've learned —

Honest Notes

Growing fast, staffed by one.

Three years of rapid growth, run as a one-person editorial operation. That's kept the project responsive and personal — and it's also the biggest constraint on what comes next.

  • Quality is non-negotiable. The goal was never just more women publishing — it's amplifying the research and researchers best placed to shape debate. That standard protects the project's credibility and its contributors'.
  • Academic writing ≠ policy writing. Many contributors are used to writing for the literature, not for a general reader. Helping authors find the public-facing question in their research takes real one-to-one time.
  • Relationships outlast articles. The project actively follows up with past contributors when new research lands or a debate turns topical — publication is a starting point, not a transaction.
  • Knowledge lives in one person's head — for now. A structured, recordable workshop on writing for policy audiences could make the editorial know-how scalable, not just personal.
“

Scaling should not simply mean publishing more. It should mean building the infrastructure that allows more economists to participate, return, and eventually engage with public audiences independently.

— Challenges and Lessons Learned
Looking Ahead · Funded Through June 2027

Building what outlasts the funding.

The final phase isn't about more articles for their own sake — it's about making sure the network, relationships, and skills built over three years keep generating impact after the project ends.

Deepen the cohort

Keep following past contributors' new work and helping them grow into confident, independent policy writers with their own media relationships.

Workshops & mentorship

Partner with VoxEU, VoxDev, and Project Syndicate on group sessions plus focused, individual editorial support.

Make the cohort visible

More presence at conferences and media hubs, showcasing contributors as a model for others considering how to engage beyond academia.

A research-focused podcast

A new audio format built around specific policy questions — and what actually happens when evidence leaves academia.

A connected cohort of women economists who continue to write, share research, recommend one another, and engage with media platforms — that's what remains after the funding ends.
